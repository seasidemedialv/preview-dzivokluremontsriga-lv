Apartment replanning without approval

Apartment replanning without approval is unauthorised construction if the work needed a construction notice, an explanatory memorandum or a building permit. A building inspector can stop the work, the building board can order the previous condition to be restored, and the owner pays the costs. It can be legalised, but that is a new construction case, and the outcome depends on the particular case.

When replanning is illegal

Not every change in an apartment is illegal replanning. For interior work, such as painting, wallpaper or replacing a floor covering, no documents are needed. The problem arises when work that needed a document was done without one.

  • A non-load-bearing wall was demolished to join rooms, without a construction notice in the Construction Information System (BIS).
  • An opening was cut or widened in a load-bearing wall without an explanatory memorandum and without the consent of more than half of the apartment owners.
  • Work with an explanatory memorandum was started before the building board's mark that the conditions for starting construction work had been met.
  • One apartment was split into two or more using a construction notice, although a notice cannot be used to create a new apartment.
  • A loggia was added to a room: the external structures of balconies and loggias are common property, so the owners' association's consent is needed.

Sources: Cabinet Regulation No. 529, sub-paragraphs 7.¹, 7.², 7.⁵ and paragraph 108; Riga municipality explanation on LV portāls 04.12.2025; Law on Residential Properties, Sections 4 and 11. Checked 11.11.2026.

How replanning is legalised

Legalisation means a new construction case in BIS after a decision of the building board. If the building board finds unauthorised construction, it adopts a decision on the deviations and sets deadlines and conditions for correcting them. The detailed procedure is different in each case.

  1. Find out what was changed Compare the existing plan with the cadastral survey file: which walls and openings are different, and whether load-bearing structures, risers or ventilation are affected.
  2. Consult the building board In Riga this is the City Development Department (Pilsētas attīstības departaments), in the Riga region the building board of the relevant municipality. There, find out which document is needed in your case.
  3. Prepare the documents For changes to a non-load-bearing wall, an architect prepares the plan of the group of premises. For an opening in a load-bearing wall, an architect and a structural engineer are needed: a survey, calculations and the solution for the opening.
  4. Obtain the owners' association's consent if common property is affected For example, an opening in a load-bearing wall needs the consent of more than half of the apartment owners, recorded as a general meeting decision or a voting record.
  5. Meet the building board's conditions The authority may also decide that the previous condition must be restored.
  6. Update the cadastral survey file After the work, the owners update the cadastral survey file so that the plan matches what is actually there.

The consequences of unauthorised construction can be remedied by any co-owner without the consent of the other co-owners. The owner of the building is responsible for remedying the consequences in the building.

Sources: Cabinet Regulation No. 529, paragraphs 9 and 162; Construction Law, Section 19(3) and (4); Riga municipality explanation on LV portāls 04.12.2025. Checked 11.11.2026.

If you are buying an apartment with a replanning

  • Ask for the cadastral survey file and compare it with the apartment as it is.
  • If the plan differs, ask the seller whether the changes have a notice or an explanatory memorandum with the building board's mark in BIS.
  • If there are no documents, expect that the costs of legalisation or of restoring the previous condition will be yours after the purchase.
  • Be especially careful in assessing openings in load-bearing walls and added loggias.

For renovation after a purchase: renovation after buying an apartment. If you are preparing an apartment for sale: renovation before selling.

Frequently asked questions

Is the new owner responsible for an old unapproved replanning?

The Construction Law states that the owner of a building is responsible for remedying the consequences of unauthorised construction, and the owner of the building pays the cost of restoring the previous condition. So before a purchase, compare the plan with the cadastral survey file.

Can the demolition of an unapproved wall be legalised?

You can try, but it is a new construction case in BIS after a decision of the building board, with deadlines and conditions. For a non-load-bearing wall an architect's plan and a construction notice are usually needed; for an opening in a load-bearing wall, a structural engineer's solution, an explanatory memorandum and the association's consent.

Can a cosmetic renovation count as unauthorised construction?

For interior work, such as painting, wallpaper or replacing a floor covering, no construction documentation is needed. Unauthorised construction is work that needed a document but did not have one.

What penalty is possible for illegal replanning?

For construction work without an explanatory memorandum or a building permit, when they are needed, for a private individual: a warning or a fine of up to 400 fine units (up to €2,000). In addition, restoring the previous condition may have to be paid for.
