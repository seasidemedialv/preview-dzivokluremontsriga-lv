Mould in a bathroom - renovation solutions

Mould in a bathroom is a sign that damp stays there longer than it should. The stain can be cleaned off, but it comes back if the cause is not dealt with: weak ventilation, water getting under the tiles or damaged joints. The renovation solution is to find which of these applies in your case and fix it.

Where to look for the cause of the damp

  1. Ventilation Hold a slip of paper by the extract grille with the door closed. If it does not move, air is not flowing.
  2. Joints Look at the joints in the corners and around the bath, shower and sink. Black, cracked or detached joints let water get under the tiles.
  3. Tiles Tap the tiles by the shower. A hollow sound and loose tiles can mean there is water under them.
  4. External wall and corners If the mould is by an external wall or in a ceiling corner, the cause can also be in the building's structures.

In series buildings, damp problems are also mentioned in lists of typical problems: for the 602 series, possible damp penetration and checking corners for mould are mentioned; for the 464 series, waterproofing problems and poor ventilation on the lower floors. Check in your own apartment whether this applies to you.

Sources: apollo.lv on series projects in Riga; nopo.lv on apartment building series. Checked 11.11.2026.

Ventilation: what the rules say

The kitchens, toilets, bathrooms and shower rooms of residential buildings are ventilated through natural extract ducts (LBN 231-15, paragraph 90.²). On a shared ventilation system, the air ducts of the kitchen and sanitary rooms may only be connected each to its own duct.

The Fire Safety Regulations currently in force require natural ventilation ducts to be cleaned and inspected at least once every 10 years, and in rooms with gas appliances at least once every five years. In an apartment building, connecting a mechanical ventilation unit to a natural ventilation duct is prohibited if the room has a gas hob or another gas appliance and there is no constant air exchange.

The ventilation duct is part of the building's engineering systems, so ask the building manager about its cleaning and inspection. If the duct is blocked, the mould will come back even after a new renovation.

Sources: Cabinet Regulation No. 310, LBN 231-15, paragraphs 85.4 and 90.²; Cabinet Regulation No. 238, sub-paragraphs 80.1, 80.2 and 89.10 (will cease to be in force on 02.01.2027). Checked 11.11.2026.

Waterproofing under tiles

If water gets under the tiles, the tiles usually have to be removed, the substrate dried and new waterproofing applied. The manufacturer's requirements show why this work cannot be done over the top:

  • Ceresit CL 51 is intended for waterproofing walls and floors before tiling in wet rooms, including on heated floors.
  • The manufacturer recommends at least two coats so that the dry layer is 0.5 mm.
  • Waterproofing tape is worked into the wall and floor joints and edges, collars are used for pipes and drains, and corner pieces for corners.
  • The substrate must be dry, load-bearing, stable and clean; crumbling surfaces are primed.

Source: Ceresit CL 51 technical data sheet LV, 11/2025 (Henkel). Checked 11.11.2026.

Joints: silicone and grout

In the Ceresit CM 17 technical data sheet, the manufacturer requires joints in corners, at the floor and at plumbing fixtures to be filled with silicone, not grout. Hard grout cracks in these places, and water goes through the crack.

For Ceresit CE 40 grout, water exposure is allowed after 24 hours, it reaches full strength after 5 days, and for the first 5 days it is washed only with clean water. If the mould is only in the joints and the tiles hold, regrouting tile joints may be enough.

Sources: Ceresit CM 17 technical data sheet 10/2026; Ceresit CE 40 technical data sheet 05/2026. Checked 11.11.2026.

Treating mould and renovation

Treating the room for mould and renovation are two separate jobs. For treating the room against mould, see dezinfekcijariga.lv. Renovation removes the cause: the joints, waterproofing, tiles and finish.

What can go wrong

  • The mould is painted over with ordinary paint, and after a couple of months it appears again.
  • New tiles are laid over old ones without finding out whether there is damp under them.
  • The joints in corners and around the bath are filled with grout instead of silicone.
  • A finish or a cupboard is put in front of the ventilation grille.

Frequently asked questions

Why does mould in a bathroom come back after cleaning?

Because the cause of the damp remains: a blocked ventilation duct, water under the tiles or damaged joints. Until the cause is fixed, the mould comes back.

Can only the silicone joints be replaced?

Yes, if the tiles hold and there is no damp under them. The manufacturer requires silicone, not grout, in corners and at plumbing fixtures.

Can a fan be connected to the ventilation duct?

In an apartment building this is prohibited if the room has a gas appliance and there is no constant air exchange. In other cases, ask the building manager.

What should you state in your request?
