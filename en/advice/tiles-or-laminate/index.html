Tiles or laminate in a kitchen and hallway?

Tiles or laminate in a kitchen and hallway is a choice between two coverings with different requirements for the subfloor, joints and moisture. Tiles are laid on adhesive and grouted; laminate is laid floating, with gaps at the walls. The choice depends on how much water and dirt reaches the floor, whether there will be underfloor heating and how the covering will connect with the neighbouring rooms.

Tiles or laminate in a kitchen

In a kitchen, the risk to the floor is water by the sink, the dishwasher and the fridge. With a tiled floor, the joints next to furniture and plumbing fixtures are filled with silicone. Ceresit CL 51 waterproofing is also intended for kitchens, if the floor needs protection under the tiles.

For a kitchen, choose a laminate that its manufacturer intends for use in a kitchen. Each manufacturer writes the water resistance and cleaning requirements in its own instructions, and they differ between products. Check this before buying, not after installation. Laminate needs a gap around pipes and thresholds; Quick-Step states that the hole for a pipe must be 20 mm larger than the pipe.

Another option is vinyl flooring, which is laid in the same way as laminate but has its own manufacturer requirements.

Tiles or laminate in a hallway

In a hallway, water and sand from shoes get onto the floor. If the hallway is large, tiles can be laid by the front door and laminate in the rest. Then the transition between the coverings is important.

In the Ceresit CN 76 technical data sheet, movement joints in the levelling compound are provided also over room thresholds, and joints in the subfloor are repeated in the levelling compound. For laminate, Quick-Step requires a gap also at thresholds and under doors. That is why the transition is planned before the floor is levelled, so that the surfaces of both coverings are at the same height.

Source: Ceresit CN 76 technical data sheet 11.25; Quick-Step laminate installation instructions LV. Checked 11.11.2026.

Underfloor heating under tiles or laminate

If you plan underfloor heating in the kitchen or hallway, each covering has its own requirements. Quick-Step states that with floor heating the subfloor surface must not be warmer than 28.8 °C and the moisture must be below 1.5%. For a cement subfloor with heating under laminate and parquet, Ceresit CN 76 gives a moisture content of up to 1.8%.

Connecting a water-based underfloor heating system to the building's heating usually affects common property, because the building's heating elements are common property, so the consent of the association is usually needed. For electric floor heating, when the electrical wiring is rebuilt, a requirement for residual current protection may apply. More on the page underfloor heating.

Sources: Quick-Step instructions LV; Ceresit CN 76 technical data sheet; Law on Residential Properties (Dzīvokļa īpašuma likums), Sections 4 and 11; LV portāls 02.07.2025 on residual current protection. Checked 11.11.2026.

Before installation

  1. Subfloor check Unevenness and moisture are measured and compared with the covering manufacturer's requirement.
  2. Levelling If needed, the floor is levelled and left until it has dried.
  3. Acclimatising the laminate Quick-Step panels acclimatise for 48 hours in unopened packaging at room temperature in the room where they will be laid.
  4. Laying and joints Tiles are grouted after the adhesive manufacturer's time (CM 17: on a floor after at least 24 hours); for laminate, gaps are left and skirting boards are fitted.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better in a kitchen: tiles or laminate?

It depends on how much water reaches the floor, what the subfloor is and whether there will be underfloor heating. For laminate, choose a product whose manufacturer intends it for a kitchen. Installation work: tiling from €24/m², laminate from €6.50/m².

Can laminate be laid on underfloor heating?

Quick-Step allows it if the subfloor surface is not warmer than 28.8 °C and the moisture is below 1.5%.

Can you lay tiles in the hallway and laminate in the room without a threshold?

You can, if the transition is planned before levelling. Laminate needs a gap at the transition, covered with a profile.
