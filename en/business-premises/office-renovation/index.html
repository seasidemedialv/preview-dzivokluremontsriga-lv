Office renovation in Riga

What is different in an office renovation compared with an apartment

An office has fewer wet rooms and more identical workstations. Most of the work is partitions, ceilings, floor and electrics, not a bathroom or kitchen. The premises may be rented, so discuss the job list, and what stays after the lease ends, with the landlord before the renovation.

The second difference is time: how long a renovation takes depends on the scope of work and the drying time of the materials.

Offices with plasterboard partitions

An open space is divided into offices or a meeting room with plasterboard partitions. The frame is made of CW profiles (studs) and UW profiles on the floor and ceiling, which are clad with plasterboard. Sound insulation material can be placed in the partition and cables run for sockets and switches. More: plasterboard partitions.

The regulations have no type of construction called “replanning of a group of rooms”: the type is chosen by the content of the work, and a designer can recommend the most suitable one. If partitions change the layout of the rooms, ask the building board or a designer which documents are needed.

Explanation by a sworn advocate on LV portāls, 13.09.2024; Cabinet Regulation No. 529 (“Ēku būvnoteikumi”, Building Construction Regulations). Checked 11.11.2026.

Ceiling, floor and electrics in an office

Suspended or plasterboard ceilings hide the cables and let you place light fittings above the workstations; they cost from €15/m². For an office floor you choose a covering that withstands chair wheels and foot traffic, for example vinyl flooring from €10/m² or laminate from €6.50/m². For laminate in large rooms Quick-Step recommends an expansion gap every 13 m.

Each workstation needs sockets and sometimes a data network point, so count the points from the desk plan; each electrical point is from €12 per point. If the electrical installation is rebuilt, it must be fitted with residual current protection, and installation is carried out under the supervision of a construction specialist with a certificate.

The order of work in an office renovation

  1. Plan Workstations, offices, the meeting room, the kitchen area and electrical points on the plan.
  2. Demolition The previous tenant's partitions, ceilings and covering, if they are being changed.
  3. Frames and cables Frames for partitions and ceilings, cables for sockets and light fittings.
  4. Cladding and finishing Plasterboard, skimming, priming and painting.
  5. Floor and doors Covering, doors, skirting boards and socket frames.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an office renovation cost per m²?

There is no single price per m² for an office renovation, because the job list differs from office to office. Unit prices: painting from €4/m², vinyl flooring from €10/m², partition from €12/m². An exact estimate can be made after seeing the premises.

Does an office renovation need approval from the building board?

Interior work without renovation, reconstruction or demolition does not need construction intent documents. If you change the layout or the type of use, find out which documents are needed from the building board or a designer.

Can partitions in an office be removed later?

A plasterboard partition can be dismantled; demolition of walls, floors and finishes is from €6/m². If you rent the premises, discuss with the landlord whether the partitions have to be removed after the lease ends.
