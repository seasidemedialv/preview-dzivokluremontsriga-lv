We do Khrushchev-era apartment renovation in Riga and the Riga region, from cosmetic to full. Cosmetic renovation starts from €100/m², full renovation from €280/m² of apartment area.

What a “Khrushchyovka” is

“Khrushchyovkas” is the name for buildings begun in the second half of the 1950s, for example the 1-317 series and similar. They are mostly 2–5 storey buildings, and the five-storey ones have no lift. The oldest were built of silicate brick, the later ones of panel blocks.

Cityreal.lv gives typical areas: a 1-room apartment 29–35 m², a 2-room 39–41 m², a 3-room about 51 m². The rooms are small and walk-through, and the sound insulation is poor. Nopo.lv writes that in Soviet-era buildings ceilings are generally around 2.5 m, but for a specific series this is not described uniformly, so measure your own apartment.

Sources: nopo.lv on Soviet-era building series, cityreal.lv on apartment types. Checked 11.11.2026.

A small apartment: what to decide before the renovation

  • Combined bathroom: in older five-storey buildings the bathroom and toilet are often combined; check your own apartment. More on this: bathroom renovation in a Khrushchyovka.
  • The kitchen is smaller than in later series. If you want to join it with a room, you must find out whether the wall is load-bearing. If the kitchen has a gas hob, it can be moved within one room in agreement with the gas supplier. Moving it to another room needs the supplier's technical conditions, a report on the flue and ventilation ducts, and a building design.
  • A walk-through room: the passage can be partitioned off with a plasterboard partition wall (from €12/m² of wall) or the door opening can be moved.
  • Sound: the walls between apartments and the floor slabs are common property; inside the apartment, insulation can be improved with finishing layers.

Non-load-bearing partitions, finishes, doors and services up to the risers belong to the apartment. Load-bearing walls, walls between apartments and risers are common property; work in load-bearing walls and walls between apartments needs the association's consent, while replacing a riser section is agreed with the building manager.

Sources: the Law on Residential Properties (Dzīvokļa īpašuma likums), Sections 3, 4 and 11; LBN 241-15 (internal natural gas pipelines), paragraphs 7–9. Checked 11.11.2026.

Khrushchev-era apartment renovation step by step

  1. Measurements and plan The estimate is based on the room areas, the wall height and the list of work.
  2. Demolition Removing the old finish and floor layers. Old vinyl floor tiles and their adhesive can contain asbestos; it cannot be determined by appearance, only in a laboratory.
  3. Wiring and pipes In many homes the wiring is from the Soviet era and worn out. New wiring is installed under the supervision of a certified construction specialist, with residual current protection for socket and lighting circuits.
  4. Walls and floors Plastering and skimming, screed or floor levelling. For a cement screed under tiles, the manufacturer Ceresit requires at least 28 days and moisture below 4%.
  5. Finishing Tiling the bathroom, painting, floor covering, doors.

How long a renovation takes depends on the scope of the work and the drying time of the materials.

Sources: LV portāls 2023 on old wiring; LBN 261-15 (paragraphs 28.1 and 55); Ceresit CM 17 technical data sheet; RSU seminar materials on asbestos. Checked 11.11.2026.

What to write in your request

  • the address and the building series or year of construction;
  • the apartment area in m² and the number of rooms;
  • a short description of each room;
  • whether the bathroom is combined or separate, and whether the kitchen has a gas hob;
  • whether you will change the wiring, pipes and riser sections;
  • whether you will choose the materials yourself.

Khrushchyovka renovation costs

  • Cosmetic renovation: from €100/m² of apartment area.
  • Full renovation: from €280/m² of apartment area.
  • Bathroom renovation (the whole job): from €3,000.

Prices are for labour. Materials are calculated separately in the estimate. Examples: a 1-room apartment of 33 m², full renovation: 33 × €280 = from €9,240 (labour). A 2-room apartment of 40 m², cosmetic renovation: 40 × €100 = from €4,000 (labour).

The price changes with the area and number of rooms, the type of renovation, the condition of the walls, floors and ceilings, whether the wiring, pipes and risers are changed, and the finish chosen. What a full renovation includes

What this work includes

  • demolition back to the structures of the walls, floors and ceilings
  • new wiring and pipes within the apartment
  • screed or floor levelling
  • wall plastering and skimming
  • all finishing: painting, tiles, floor covering, doors and skirting boards

What is calculated separately

  • materials, sanitaryware and the doors themselves
  • replacing a riser section (from €100 per riser) and radiators
  • replanning documents and approval
  • construction waste removal
  • work in common property: the facade, the external structures of balconies, the stairwell

For construction waste removal, see buvgruzuizvesanariga.lv.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a full renovation of a 1-room Khrushchyovka cost?

The work starts from €280/m² of apartment area: for a 33 m² apartment that is from €9,240. Materials are added in the estimate. The total depends on the condition of the walls and floors and on whether a riser section is replaced.

Can the bathroom and toilet be combined in a Khrushchyovka?

In some buildings they are already combined, so first check your own apartment. If they are separate, the partition between them is usually part of the apartment, but first check that it is not load-bearing. More: combining the bathroom and toilet.

Is it worth changing the wiring in a Khrushchyovka?

If the wiring is from the Soviet era, a full renovation is the time to decide, because afterwards the walls will already have a new finish. The Fire Safety Regulations currently in force require the wiring to be inspected every 10 years, and the inspection report shows its condition.

How do you gain more space in a small Khrushchyovka kitchen?

Two possible solutions are combining the kitchen with a room or moving the door opening. In both cases you must first find out whether the wall is load-bearing, and with a gas hob you must talk to the gas supplier.
