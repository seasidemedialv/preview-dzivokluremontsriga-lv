What the building series says about a renovation

The series does not determine the renovation by itself, but it says a lot about the walls, floor slabs and services. It affects whether a partition can be moved, how much work the old wiring and pipes need and which problems concern the whole building rather than your apartment.

The apartment owner owns the non-load-bearing partitions, the finish of walls, ceilings and floors, doors and services up to the common-property risers. Load-bearing walls, walls between apartments, floor slabs, external walls, windows in external structures and the building's services are common property. In their own apartment, an owner may carry out construction work without the agreement of other owners, as far as it does not affect common property or other apartments; if it does, the consent of the apartment owners' association is needed.

Law on Residential Properties, Section 3(2), Section 4(1) and Section 11. Checked 11.11.2026.

Which series page to choose

602 series apartment renovation: expanded clay concrete panel buildings from the mid-1970s to the early 1980s, mostly 9 storeys. The internal walls are usually load-bearing, so changes to openings need a structural engineer's solution.

119 series apartment renovation: reinforced concrete panel buildings from the early 1980s to roughly the mid-1990s. For the 119 series it has been published that the external walls are usually load-bearing; which walls are load-bearing can be said only from the building design or a structural engineer's opinion.

103 series apartment renovation: brick external walls, and it has been published that the partitions are usually non-load-bearing, of aerated concrete or expanded clay concrete; which walls are load-bearing is shown by the building design. Mostly 5 storeys. The bathroom and toilet are usually separate.

104 series apartment renovation: 9-, 12- or 16-storey buildings with aerated concrete external walls and reinforced concrete internal load-bearing walls. In a one-room apartment the bathroom and toilet are usually combined.

467 series apartment renovation: 9-storey reinforced concrete large-panel buildings from the late 1970s and the 1980s, where practically all the walls are load-bearing. Worn pipes and wiring often have to be dealt with.

464 series apartment renovation: 5-storey panel buildings with gypsum concrete partitions, built since the early 1960s. Typical issues are waterproofing, risers and ventilation.

316 and 318 series apartment renovation: sand-lime brick buildings from 1956–1964 with 2–5 storeys. Lithuanian project apartment renovation: this is often what 464 series buildings are called. Khrushchyovka renovation: buildings from the second half of the 1950s with small, walk-through rooms.

Older buildings have their own pages. Stalin-era apartment renovation: masonry buildings with thick brick walls and high ceilings. Pre-war building apartment renovation: masonry tenement buildings with mostly wooden floor slabs. Wooden house apartment renovation: wooden tenement buildings, for example in Āgenskalns and Čiekurkalns.

The table shows what public sources say about the series. A particular building can be different, so check the walls, the bathroom and the ceiling height in your own apartment and write in the request what you found; you can send a plan or photos to the specialist when they contact you.

The facade, joints and roof are a matter for the whole building

In a Ministry of Economics study, the 104 and 119 series buildings had no typical defects, but individual shortcomings were found in the joints, balcony fixings and water collecting on the roof. In a study of the 464 series buildings they were found safe for continued use. External walls, the roof and the external structures of balconies are common property, and their repair is decided by the association together with the building manager.

There are support programmes for renovating a whole building: “Rīga renovē” co-finances an energy audit, a technical survey and a technical design, while Altum loans are intended for the whole apartment building. These programmes are for the whole building. If a joint leaks or there is damp in a corner, first report it to the building manager, and only then renew the finish.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find out which series my apartment is in?

If you do not know the series, write the address in the request, and the year of construction if it is known. For a renovation, information about the walls and services in your apartment is more important than the series number.

Does a renovation in a series building need a permit?

Documents are not needed for an interior project without reconstruction (Cabinet Regulation No. 529, sub-paragraph 7.1); the municipality explains that this applies, for example, to painting, wallpaper and replacing the floor covering. If you demolish a non-load-bearing wall, in Riga a construction notice is submitted, and for an opening in a load-bearing wall an explanatory memorandum is needed. More detail: does a renovation need a permit.

May a wall be demolished in a panel building?

It depends on whether the wall is load-bearing. For a non-load-bearing wall, according to the Riga municipality, an architect prepares a plan and submits a notice. An opening in a load-bearing wall needs an architect and a structural engineer, an explanatory memorandum and the consent of more than half of the apartment owners. More detail: may a wall in an apartment be demolished.

Who pays for riser replacement in a series building?

A riser is the common property of the apartment owners. Each owner covers the costs of managing the building according to their share, on the basis of the association's decision, and riser replacement is usually paid for in the same way. More detail: risers as common property.

Can I glaze a loggia in a series building without documents?

Yes, if the work matches a loggia glazing model sketch approved by the municipality or a facade sketch agreed with the building board (Cabinet Regulation No. 529, sub-paragraph 7.4.7). The annex to the Riga regulations has standard sketches for the 103, 104, 119, 464, 467 and 602 series.
