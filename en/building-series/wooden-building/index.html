Riga's wooden apartment houses

Āgenskalns has about 600 wooden buildings. In 1913 Čiekurkalns had 802 residential buildings, mainly wooden, and Maskavas forštate has wooden buildings preserved from the early 19th century. There are also wooden apartment houses in Grīziņkalns, Torņakalns and Zasulauks. They are low-rise buildings, but the number of floors, the layout and the condition of the floor slabs differ from building to building.

In the request, describe the wall finishing, the floors and where the wiring and pipes run.

Sources: deepbaltic.com on Āgenskalns (2016); apkaimes.lv on the history of Čiekurkalns. Checked 11.11.2026.

What belongs to the apartment and what to the whole building

If a wooden building is divided into apartment properties, the external walls, roof, windows in the external structures, load-bearing walls and floor slabs are common property. The apartment owns the non-load-bearing partitions, the finishing of walls, ceilings and floors, the doors and the utilities up to the risers. Work on common property needs the consent of the apartment owners' association.

In 2025 LSM described a wooden apartment building on Kuldīgas iela which, after renovation, has new windows and doors, new facade cladding, a partly new roof and repaired water supply and sewage, while the decorative elements were preserved. That is work on the whole building, decided by the association; the apartment's interior work can be planned independently of it.

Sources: Law on Residential Properties, Sections 3, 4 and 11; LSM 05.03.2025. Checked 11.11.2026.

Wet rooms, electricity and windows in a wooden house

  • A bathroom or toilet is placed above similar rooms; above the living rooms of the apartment below, sanitary rooms are permitted if the requirements for sound insulation, waterproofing, thermal insulation, fire safety, ventilation and safety are met.
  • Waterproofing: the manufacturer of Ceresit CL 51 requires a dry, load-bearing and stable base and recommends at least two coats. On a wooden floor this means a stable base has to be prepared first.
  • Electrical installation: a new installation is carried out under the supervision of a certified construction specialist, and when it is rebuilt, residual current protection is needed.
  • Windows in external structures: replacement is agreed with the building manager; the association may decide that its consent is also needed. No documents are needed in buildings of the second and third group if the window division, proportions and frame colour are kept.

Sources: LBN 200-21, paragraph 136; Ceresit CL 51 technical sheet 11/2025; LBN 261-15; LV portāls 02.07.2025; Cabinet Regulation No. 529, sub-paragraph 7.4.3. Checked 11.11.2026.

The order of work

  1. Decisions What to keep and what to change, where the wiring and pipes run.
  2. Demolition The old finishing layers; what to keep (plank floor, doors, trim) is decided beforehand.
  3. Utilities Electrical wiring with residual current protection, pipes within the apartment.
  4. Walls and floors Plasterboard lining or plastering, the floor base or restoring a wooden floor.
  5. Finishing Skimming and painting, tiles in wet rooms, floor covering.

How long a renovation takes depends on the scope of work and the drying time of the materials.

What to write in the request

  • the address and the building series or year of construction;
  • the apartment's floor area in m² and the number of rooms;
  • the condition of each room;
  • what you want to keep: a plank floor, old doors, a stove or trim;
  • whether you will change the wiring, pipes and riser sections;
  • whether you will choose the materials yourself.

What a full renovation includes

What this work includes

  • demolition to the structure of the walls, floors and ceilings;
  • new wiring and pipes within the apartment;
  • screed or floor levelling;
  • wall plastering and skimming;
  • all finishing: painting, tiles, floor covering, doors and skirting boards.

What is calculated separately

  • the materials, plumbing and doors themselves;
  • replacing a riser section (from €100 per riser) and radiators;
  • replanning documents and approvals.

Frequently asked questions

Can a bathroom with tiles be built in a wooden house apartment?

It can, if the base is dry, load-bearing and stable and the building code requirements for wet rooms above other rooms are met. On a wooden floor the base has to be sorted out first, and only then the waterproofing and tiles.

Can the owner of one apartment replace the windows in a wooden house?

The owner may replace windows in external structures at their own expense, in agreement with the building manager; the association may decide that its consent is also needed. Facade and roof work is decided by the whole association.

Can the old stove be kept in a wooden house?

Write it in the request. The finishing work around the stove can be planned so that it is kept; the condition of the stove and the chimney is assessed by other specialists.
